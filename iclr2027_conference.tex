\begin{abstract}
Recent advancements in Reinforcement Learning (RL) have heavily adopted World Models and Diffusion-based techniques; however, they often suffer from prohibitive computational costs. In this paper, we first investigate the common assumption that the performance bottleneck of existing models lies primarily in their representation capacity (e.g., poor image reconstruction). Through rigorous probing analysis, we provide evidence suggesting that existing lightweight encoders can already capture robust object-level information. This implies that representation capacity may not be the sole limitation, prompting us to investigate other structural candidates. Among these, we identify the optimization process of the Actor-Critic architecture as a major, independent bottleneck, characterized by extreme performance variance across different random seeds. To mitigate this specific issue, we propose FLASH (Fast Latent Anchor Search with Hashing), a lightweight and efficient plug-and-play module that directly aids Actor-Critic optimization. By seamlessly integrating into various RL baselines—ranging from lightweight encoder-based models to heavy diffusion-based models—FLASH elevates their performance significantly while preserving their original frames per second (FPS). Crucially, our approach acts as a powerful "Exploitation Amplifier," maximizing the evaluation scores of seeds that successfully explore, all with negligible computational overhead.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Recently, the integration of Diffusion models into Reinforcement Learning (RL) has become increasingly popular, yielding strong performance in various benchmarks \todo{DIAMOND 등 Diffusion 기반 RL 최신 논문 인용}. However, this trend has brought about a significant drawback: massive computational costs and high latency during both training and inference. This raises a fundamental question: \textit{Are these heavy computational overheads truly necessary, or are we addressing the wrong bottleneck?}

In this work, we suggest that the current singular focus on enhancing representation quality through heavy generative models may overlook other critical components of the RL pipeline. By observing extreme performance variance across different random seeds in standard setups, we hypothesize that the inherent instability in the Actor-Critic optimization process itself constitutes a major, independent bottleneck. 

Our contributions are threefold:
First, we provide analytical evidence suggesting the sufficiency of existing lightweight representations in certain environments, demonstrating that poor visual reconstruction does not necessarily equate to a loss of underlying semantic information.
Second, having established that representations are not the sole limitation, we identify the Actor-Critic optimization process as a distinct and major bottleneck hindering RL performance, evidenced by extreme seed-to-seed variance.
Finally, to mitigate this specific optimization bottleneck, we propose FLASH, an efficient retrieval mechanism that augments the Actor-Critic architecture. By effectively identifying and retrieving pivotal past experiences, our method stabilizes learning and acts as a powerful exploitation amplifier, achieving state-of-the-art results with minimal computational overhead.

\section{Are Heavy Representations Necessary?: Decoding the Sufficiency of Encoders}
\label{sec:representation}

\subsection{The Myth of Reconstruction Quality}
A common fallacy in recent model-based RL research is the assumption that blurry decoded images imply a lack of information in the latent space. We argue that this is merely a rendering failure of the decoder.

As qualitative evidence, we observe the \textit{temporal persistence of objects} in models like DreamerV3 and Drama \todo{해당 논문 인용}. For instance, when a submarine approaches a fish, the fish may blur or disappear from the decoded frame, only to reappear perfectly in its expected trajectory in subsequent frames. This phenomenon proves that the ontological information of the object was never lost in the latent state $z$; it was simply not rendered by the decoder at time $t$. Therefore, relying on reconstruction quality is a deceptive metric for evaluating the true capacity of an encoder.

\subsection{Preliminary Observation: The Cosine Similarity Clue}
To formally investigate the encoder's sensitivity to small details, we designed a motivating experiment. We passed two nearly identical image crops through the encoder: the only difference being the presence or absence of a single small diver in the frame.

Interestingly, the cosine similarity between the extracted latent states from these two images was \todo{여기에 실제 측정한 코사인 유사도 수치를 적어주세요. 예: 0.85}. If the lightweight encoder had completely ignored the diver, the similarity would have been exactly 1.0. The fact that it is strictly less than 1.0 provides a strong clue that the encoder successfully detects and maps this minute pixel difference into the latent space, motivating our subsequent probing analysis.

\subsection{Probing the Latent Space}
To verify exactly what information the latent state $z$ contains, we conducted a probing experiment on the Seaquest environment, known for its extreme sparse rewards and tiny objects. We detached the pre-trained encoder (stopping gradient flow) and trained a lightweight MLP to predict ground-truth RAM states directly from $z$.

Our results reveal that the MLP can predict the underlying game state with astonishing accuracy. Specifically, it achieved \todo{화면 하단 잠수부 개수 분류 정확도 수치 (예: 99.5\%)} accuracy for counting the number of divers at the bottom, \todo{화면 상 잠수부 존재 여부 분류 정확도 수치} accuracy for detecting the presence of a diver on screen, and a mean squared error of \todo{x,y 좌표 예측 오차 수치} for predicting the exact $(x, y)$ coordinates of the diver. This confirms that the latent space already encapsulates perfect semantic information, irrespective of the decoder's visual output.

\subsection{Robustness to Spatial Noise: Refuting the Blurring Hypothesis}
Recent works advocating for Diffusion models \todo{DIAMOND 논문 인용} hypothesize that standard encoders fail because they "blur" or entangle spatial information. To refute this, we injected heavy random noise into the background spaces of the original images, leaving only the diver's position intact.

If the blurring hypothesis were true, the extreme noise in other spatial locations would entangle with the diver's features, degrading the probing accuracy. However, our probing MLP, taking the noise-injected $z$ as input, still maintained \todo{노이즈 주입 후 유지된 잠수부 위치/개수 정확도 수치} accuracy. This demonstrates that the encoder possesses robust disentanglement capabilities, completely debunking the claim that spatial blurring is the root cause of RL failures.

\section{Beyond Representations: The Actor-Critic Optimization Bottleneck}
\label{sec:bottleneck}

\subsection{High Variance Across Random Seeds}
If representation learning were fundamentally flawed, the agent would universally fail to learn. However, we observe extreme variance in performance across different random seeds.

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:seed_variance}, the agent succeeds in some seeds and fails entirely in others. This highly stochastic outcome is a classic hallmark of unstable policy and value function optimization in RL, strongly indicating that the Actor-Critic training dynamics present a distinct and major bottleneck independent of representation capacity.

\subsection{The Challenge of Delayed Rewards and Value Spikes}
Environments like Seaquest or Frostbite often require the agent to complete complex sub-goals (e.g., collecting multiple divers or building an igloo) before receiving a massive reward. In such scenarios, the agent may occasionally stumble upon a breakthrough during random exploration, causing a sudden, massive spike in the true state value. Traditional Actor-Critic architectures struggle to efficiently stabilize and learn from these rare, high-variance value spikes, often losing the critical exploratory signal in the noise of standard transitions.

In environments featuring distinct sub-goals and episodic rewards like Frostbite, Asterix, and Qbert, FLASH successfully captures rare value spikes, resulting in an explosive performance increase. Conversely, in environments characterized by dense, continuous rewards or pure long-horizon exploration (e.g., MsPacman, Seaquest), the performance gain is more bounded. This perfectly aligns with our design: FLASH specifically mitigates the Actor-Critic bottleneck associated with sudden positive value shifts (breakthroughs), but does not artificially solve fundamental exploration challenges or alter dense-reward dynamics. Overall, the results prove that our approach significantly stabilizes the RL optimization process where value-spike bottlenecks exist.

A key property of FLASH is its ability to act as an "Exploitation Amplifier." As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:exploitation_amplifier}, if a seed manages to achieve a high episode score (e.g., $>800$) even once during early exploration, our method aggressively exploits this trajectory, pulling the overall evaluation score up to converge near the maximum achieved episode score. Conversely, for seeds that completely fail to explore (e.g., $<300$ points), there are no high-quality past experiences to retrieve, resulting in no artificial performance gain. This demonstrates that FLASH does not magically solve hardcore exploration, but ensures that rare, successful exploratory signals are never lost and are optimally absorbed by the policy.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
In this paper, we critique the recent trend of obsessing over visual representation quality in RL. We demonstrate that existing lightweight encoders already possess sufficient capacity. By shifting focus to the Actor-Critic optimization process as a major, independent bottleneck, we proposed FLASH, an efficient and low-overhead retrieval method. Our plug-and-play approach successfully amplifies rare exploratory successes and universally elevates the performance of existing RL baselines—including both lightweight and heavy diffusion-based models—while maintaining extreme computational efficiency.
